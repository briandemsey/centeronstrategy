I  ·  Our Approach

A view from the center.

Center Of The Nation is a strategy consulting firm in the northern Black Hills of South Dakota, a short drive from the geographic center of the United States. Stand at that point, 44°58′ north, 103°46′ west, and every direction lies open before you. Nothing pulls you east or west, north or south. That is where we begin every engagement.

Three hundred sixty degrees

A strategy is only as good as the view behind it. Before we recommend anything, we look in every direction: customers and competitors, employees and owners, regulators and critics, the market as it is and as it is likely to become. We are neither left nor right. We hold no house view, follow no ideology, and have no product to sell. Our only interest is the decision in front of you.

Well researched

We start with the evidence, not the conclusion. We go to primary sources, build the numbers ourselves, and test them against the goals they have to support. When the facts are uncertain, we say so plainly and show how much it matters. You will always know what we know, what we don't, and how we got from one to the other.

Thought out

Good advice takes time. Our work is done by senior people who think a problem through rather than run it through a template. Every recommendation comes with its reasoning laid out, so you can challenge it, defend it to your board, and act on it with confidence.

Far from the big cities

We work from the northern Black Hills, a long way from the conference rooms of New York, Boston, and Washington, and from the consensus that forms inside them. Distance brings perspective. Out here, the horizon is wide, the noise is low, and there is room to think. We meet clients wherever they are, in person or across the table on a screen, and we bring that clarity with us.

The balance point

The geographic center is the one place where all fifty states are held in balance. It is not a compromise between them. It is the point from which the whole can be seen. That is the view we offer our clients.

III  ·  Where We Help

Measure. Set goals. Design paths.

Every city and every business has strengths that already work. We measure them, help you set goals for the future, and design the paths that meet or exceed those goals.

Measure the strengths

We begin with a baseline of what the city or business already does well, drawn from its own data and published sources, with the measures stated plainly.

Set the goals

With the board and its leaders, we define where the community or company wants to be, and by when. A few goals, each with a measure and a date.

Design the paths

For each goal we lay out the routes that meet or exceed it, with the steps, partners, and checkpoints along the way, and we report progress on a regular schedule.

Education: what the future will require

The skills employers need will keep changing, and that asks a great deal of the university, the schools, and the adults who retrain. We measure how well programs fit local demand, set goals for the careers graduates can build close to home, and design the links between classroom and employer.

Health: providers and consumers

Healthcare is both a service a community depends on and an industry that employs it. We measure access to care and the condition of the provider workforce, set goals for both, and design ways to recruit and keep providers and to reach consumers closer to home.

Financial products and services

The way people save, borrow, insure, and pay is changing quickly. We measure how well local institutions serve households and businesses today, set goals for access to capital and financial security across a lifetime, and design how a community keeps pace with new products and services without leaving anyone behind.

Real estate development

What gets built decides what a place becomes. We measure the land, the housing supply, and the community’s own plan, set goals for the mix of housing, commercial, and educational space, and design phased development that fits the plan and gives builders and investors clear expectations.

IV  ·  The Heartland

America’s next growth opportunity.

In October 2026 the McKinsey Institute for Economic Mobility and Heartland Forward published America’s Next Growth Opportunity: An Investment-Ready Heartland. Its argument is one we see every day from the middle of the country.

The report defines the heartland as twenty states across the center of the United States, South Dakota among them. Together they would rank as the third largest economy in the world, yet they draw only a small share of the nation’s investment capital.

  • 35% Of U.S. GDP. The heartland produces more than a third of the nation’s economic output.
  • 39% Of Americans. More than a third of the country lives there.
  • ~10% Of venture capital. Yet it received only about a tenth of U.S. venture investment from 2019 to 2024, and less than a third of fixed-capital lending.
  • 60% Of smaller cities. More than 60 percent of the nation’s 538 micropolitan areas are in the heartland.

Readiness, not just capital

The authors find that communities with similar assets often see very different results. What separates them is not what they have but how well they organize it into opportunities an investor can act on. The report measures this readiness in three dimensions: the economy, the community, and coordination among the institutions that serve it.

Why it matters to us

This is the work we were built for. Center Of The Nation helps communities, employers, and investors in the heartland take an honest measure of where they stand, decide where to focus, and make the case for investment with evidence rather than optimism.

Figures are from the report. Read the full report at mckinsey.com.

V  ·  The Name

44°58′ N 103°46′ W.

About twenty miles north of Belle Fourche, South Dakota, in a pasture in Butte County, lies the geographic center of the United States: all fifty states, from Maine to Hawaii and Alaska.

Its coordinates are 44°58′ north, 103°46′ west. Written without the symbols, they become this address: 4458N 10346W. The firm takes its name from the place.

There is no town at the center, only a survey marker, a flag, and open prairie in every direction. It is a fitting reminder of how we work: no pull toward any coast or camp, and a clear view of the whole.
